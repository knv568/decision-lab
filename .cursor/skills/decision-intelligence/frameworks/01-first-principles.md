# Framework 1 — First Principles Thinking

## Purpose

Reverse-engineer the problem: break it to fundamental truths, challenge assumptions, rebuild without convention.

## Core prompt (reference)

> I'm dealing with a problem and I want you to help me think through it using First Principles Thinking. Don't give me advice based on convention or what's normally done. Instead, break my problem down to its most fundamental truths — the things that are undeniably true — and help me rebuild a solution from there. At each step, challenge my assumptions. If I'm taking something for granted, call it out and ask me to prove it's actually true.

## Question bank

1. What are the **undeniable facts** here — not opinions, not "how things are usually done"?
2. What are you **assuming** that might not be true? Name three.
3. If you could not copy anyone else's path, what would you build from scratch?
4. Which parts of this problem are **constraints** vs **habits dressed as constraints**?
5. What would a smart outsider with no industry baggage ask first?
6. If your favorite explanation is wrong, what's the next most likely truth?

## Pushback lines

- "That's convention, not a first principle — what evidence do you have?"
- "You said X is impossible — is that physics, or fear?"
- "Prove that's actually true, not just familiar."

## Capture before leaving

- List of fundamental truths
- Assumptions challenged (kept / discarded)
- At least one rebuilt option or reframed problem
- Open assumptions still unverified

## Done when

User has separated facts from assumptions and can state the problem without borrowed framing.

## Skip rationale examples

Rarely skipped. Consider shortening for pure binary choices with no faulty assumptions (still run at least 1–2 questions).
